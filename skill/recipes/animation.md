# Animation: Mixamo to a game character

An all-Node pipeline: Blender is not needed for this one.

1. **Download** on mixamo.com (signed in by the owner): character X Bot, FBX Binary, T-pose, 30 fps, no keyframe reduction. A motion pack downloads as one zip. Register it ([assets.md](assets.md)). The raw files never enter a repo.
2. **Retarget.** Write one retarget script per target rig. Its method:
   - one bone map, Mixamo to your skeleton's logical names (strip numeric suffixes that exporters add, and map them back);
   - rest frames calibrated once from bone endpoints;
   - uniform leg-length scale for hips travel;
   - one constant floor offset;
   - root travel split out of the hips.
   three 0.18's FBXLoader misreads some Mixamo footers; trim after the top-level node list. Import three from the game's own `node_modules`. Combat clips stay in place, apart from the vertical drop of a fall.
3. **Bake** into the game model with your project's packer (texture/vertex repack, finger curl for a sword grip, credit in `asset.extras`). Only baked clips ship. Then shrink the character with `node tools/optimize-gltf.mjs in.glb out.glb --profile skinned` ([assets.md](assets.md) step 4), and review the sheet again. One-shot clips can be stored with key reduction (0.2 deg) and int16 rotations: six clips cost about 8 KB.
4. **Review** each clip as a rendered sheet ([verify.md](verify.md)): the sword stays in the hand, feet neither sink nor float, and no joint flips.
5. Done when the game model holds the new clips under game names, the budget test passes, and the review sheets are saved and looked at.

**A Mixamo character as is (no retarget):** download the character FBX Binary, T-pose, with skin, and each clip FBX without skin, "In Place", 30 fps. Then
`node tools/prepare-mixamo-npc.mjs <char.fbx> <idle.fbx> <walk.fbx> <run.fbx> <out.glb> --height <m> [--tint-region Mat=x0,y0,x1,y1] [--ramp #c0,#c1,.. --keep x0,y0,x1,y1]` (`--ramp` recolours the whole texture by luminance except the `--keep` rectangles; clips become Idle_A/Walking_A/Running_A on the Mixamo rig, JPEG textures, quantized, alpha meshes dropped; `--tint-region` splits atlas rectangles into a named grey material a game can recolour), then `optimize-gltf.mjs --profile skinned --texture-size 768`, then a sheet with `render-sheet.mjs` ([verify.md](verify.md)).

**A garment over the body:** add `--tabard #field,#trim,#emblem` to prepare-mixamo-npc. It builds a skinned, double-sided cloth tabard (front and back panels shaped on the bind-pose body with an offset, joined over the shoulders, slit from the crotch so each flap follows its thigh; weights copied from the nearest torso/thigh vertices with arm, head and lower-leg bones folded away; about 1.4K triangles and 15 KB with its own texture). Then `node tools/check-garment.mjs <glb> --material Tabard` (body vertices that cross the cloth, per clip frame, along the cloth normal; exits 1 over `--max-cm`, default 3) and a back-view sheet with `render-sheet.mjs --yaw 180`.

**Secondary motion:** `prepare-mixamo-npc` also bakes a custom uint8 vertex attribute `_CLOTH` (0 pinned .. 1 free; the tabard gets it from its belt to its hem, `--cloth x0,y0,x1,y1,<Bone>,<offsetFracH>,<max>,<Material>` picks loose cloth on the textured body mesh by UV rectangle, one per flap or cowl) and `--albedo-floor 0.035` lifts a near-black costume texture so its 5th-percentile linear luminance reaches the floor (affine, in linear light; dyed wool is 0.04-0.06, never black). The game moves `_CLOTH` vertices in the vertex shader; check the worst case with `check-garment.mjs <glb> --material Tabard --spring sx,sz,billow` for +-sx and +-sz (metres, e.g. 0.14,0,0.045). `optimize-gltf` keeps the custom attribute. Measure each model's own strides (planted-foot speed) and walk/run phase offset; never scale another character's strides by height.

**Foot IK, foot lock and weight on top of a stride-synced clip:** no root motion; the capsule keeps moving the body. Per frame after the mixer: capture the animated feet (contact = ankle within 3 cm of its lowest height), lean/bank/settle the spine and pelvis, then pin planted feet in world x/z and solve each leg with `tools/two-bone-ik.mjs` (keep the foot's animated world rotation). Rules that held up in the sheets: a pin released by stretch becomes a short lifted step (never a glide), and only while the other foot is planted; lift-off releases add no extra lift (it popped the knee); a swing foot looks 0.12 s ahead for a rise in the ground; the pelvis drops at once when a target would ask more than 98.5% of the leg (a pelvis on a low-pass lagged off a sill and snapped the knee straight). Refresh only the chain's world matrices (root to toes and head), never `updateWorldMatrix(true, ...)` per bone: that halved the cost per actor.

**Combat weight on top of duel clips:** keep the encounter logic pure; a read-only listener queues knocks and falls, and a post-animation layer bends bones after the mixer, so hit windows and timing never move. Guard and strike: find the held meshes by single-bone skin weighting, take their principal axes by PCA, then reach the shield and sword hands with two-bone IK to targets built in the chest frame, weighted by the guard/strike shares, recovery blended back in at 72-100% of the strike clip; cap the torso bow at 0.3 rad. Flinch: a damped spring (omega 15, zeta 0.45) on spine and head about up x hit direction. Stagger: move the body kinematically along an ease-out cubic (0.3 s), with the distance clamped against the world's solid boxes first, and let foot IK step the planted feet after it. Falls are procedural, not a ragdoll, with only the dropped weapon as a rigid body that is frozen once asleep.

When this recipe has run twice on different characters, promote the retarget script into `tools/retarget-mixamo.mjs`, with the rig profile as a parameter.
